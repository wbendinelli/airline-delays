# Decisions

Architecture decision records for `airline-delays`. One entry per decision, short, dated. Superseded or amended entries are kept and marked. The decision text is English because this file is part of the public repository. The definitions recorded in ADR-0001, 0002, 0003, 0005, 0012 and 0013 are the article's own -- Bendinelli, Bettini & Oliveira (2016, *Transportation Research Part A* 85, 39-52, doi 10.1016/j.tra.2016.01.001) -- restated here by its first author; ADR-0008 records a delay threshold the authors' scripts disagree on, and ADR-0017 is this repository's reading of IAC 1504. The two panels of this repository share the universe, the node map and the carrier sets (ADR-0020).

Evidence base: `docs/notes/` (Portuguese).

## ADR-0000 — VRA licence and redistribution (2026-09-05)

**Context.** ANAC's website footer states CC BY-ND 3.0 for "all content of this site"; no ANAC page declares a licence for the dataset itself. Read literally, ND forbids derivatives.

**Evidence.** The federal open-data catalogue declares `Licença: Creative Commons Attribution` for the dataset "Voos e operações aéreas - Voo Regular Ativo (VRA)" (https://dados.gov.br/dados/conjuntos-dados/dadosabertos-areas-de-atuacao-voos-e-operacoes-aereas-voo-regular-ativo-vra, read 2026-09-05; catalogued 2019-03-01, metadata updated 2024-01-25, responsible unit GOPE). Licence version is not stated.

**Decision.** Treat the VRA as CC BY. Attribute as "ANAC, Voo Regular Ativo (VRA), via dados.gov.br". Redistribute derived data with attribution. File an e-SIC request for written confirmation in parallel; do not block on it. Record the catalogue page and read date in `README.md` and `datapackage.json`.

## ADR-0001 — Nodes: Viracopos inside São Paulo (2026-09-05)

**Basis.** The article's flight count `f` is a metropolitan count: São Paulo is Congonhas, Guarulhos and Viracopos together, although the tariff base labels Viracopos "Campinas". The node map is the article's.

**Decision.** Nodes are the 24 single-airport capitals plus MRSP = SBSP + SBGR + SBKP, MRRJ = SBGL + SBRJ, MRBH = SBBH + SBCF. Every table carries `o_icao` and `d_icao` next to `o` and `d`, so airport-level work never requires re-extraction.

## ADR-0002 — Universe (2026-09-05)

**Basis.** The article's `f` counts the scheduled flights of line types N, R and E with DI 0, realised and cancelled (`prcanc = fl_can / f`); extra and return flights are outside it.

**Decision.** Replication universe: line types N, R, E; DI 0; realised and cancelled flights. Prediction universe: realised flights of the same universe; extras and return flights become features, never filters. Column names carry the universe; a test forbids silent mixing.

## ADR-0003 — Airline groups, mergers and classes (2026-09-05)

**Decision.** `data/external/groups.csv` is a dated table: `airline, group, start, end, class, source, url`. Its initial content is the article's grouping: Varig group (VRG, VLO, VRN, NES, RSL) becomes Gol from 2007-04; Webjet becomes Gol from 2011-11; Pantanal becomes TAM from 2009-12; TTL becomes Trip from 2007-11; Trip becomes Azul from 2012-05. Classes: FSC = TAM, Varig, Vasp, Transbrasil; LCC = Gol, Azul, Webjet; regional = Trip, Passaredo, Pantanal and the rest. Open cases decided: Varig after 2007-04 counts as Gol (LCC); Webjet 2005-2011 is an independent LCC; Avianca Brasil (Oceanair) is FSC. Every row cites a source (CADE decision, ANAC act, or press of record) with a confidence grade.

## ADR-0004 — Grain and storage format (2026-09-05)

**Evidence.** At daily grain 81% of route-day cells have fewer than 10 flights and a 22% prevalence has a standard error of 0.145; weekly 18% and 0.055; monthly 2.6% and 0.027. The full flight table is 250-290 MB in partitioned parquet.

**Decision.** The record grain is the flight. The canonical fact table is `group x route x month`. Route-month, city-month and airline-city-month tables are generated by `aggregate()` and tested for additivity; order statistics are recomputed from flights at each grain. Weekly and daily aggregates exist on demand only and are never committed. Storage: parquet, zstd level 9, tight types, partitioned by year; canonical above 2 MB, `csv.gz` below; both panels ship in both formats. Flight tables are regenerated, not committed. No DVC; no `.duckdb` files in git.

## ADR-0005 — Delay-cause code taxonomy (2026-09-05)

**Basis.** The article's `prwheather` merges weather with closed or restricted airports, and its dominant code is AR ("aeroporto com restrições operacionais"); `princident` = DF, DG, HB, MA, TD; `pr_connc` = RA, "aircraft connection", which the article describes as waiting for connecting passengers.

**Decision.** Keep the article's three columns exactly. Add a second taxonomy alongside: weather (WO, WT, WA, WR, WS, WI, AM; cancellations XO, XT, XS); airport closed or restricted (AI, AJ, AR; XI, XJ, XM); aircraft rotation (RA, RI, RM); technical (TD, DF, DG, MA, HB); operational and air traffic (AT, FP, GF, AS, AF, AG); authorised (HA, HD, OA, XA, XB); other. The AR finding is documented in `docs/notes/features.md`.

## ADR-0006 — Name, owner and language (2026-09-05)

**Decision.** Repository `airline-delays` under `wbendinelli`, class Research, tier C. README, code identifiers, column names and docstrings in English, as the `research` profile of the SAPIANS doclint requires. Reports (Typst) and the didactic material in Portuguese, recorded as a deliberate exception in `CLAUDE.md`, following the precedent of `citation-audit`. Amended by ADR-0021: the entry layer is bilingual.

## ADR-0007 — Congestion (2026-09-05)

**Evidence.** The BNDES/McKinsey 2010 study reports airport capacity in passengers per year, not movements per hour; hourly declared capacity lives in ANAC's seasonal capacity declarations, not yet collected. An internal proxy (day-hours above the city's own 90th percentile within the year) already exists.

**Decision.** Ship both: the internal p90 proxy now, and the declared-capacity definition when the ANAC declarations are collected. The reconstruction panel carries no `prcongested` until then; the article's estimation panel carries the authors' own `prcongested`, `dailyflcong` and `dailyflncong` (ADR-0020).

## ADR-0008 — Outlier rule and sign of delays (2026-09-05)

**Evidence.** The authors' scripts used 313.25 minutes in one and 117.10 (arrival) with 111.75 (departure) in another; the article's panel does not trim `f`; FSC minutes keep early arrivals negative and trim outliers by a rule the scripts do not state.

**Decision.** Outlier threshold is a named parameter with default 313.25 minutes; early arrivals stay negative; the replication report carries a sensitivity table of the main coefficient across thresholds.

## ADR-0009 — Evaluation design for delay prediction (2026-09-05)

**Evidence.** 43.6% of arrivals were late in 2007 and 15.7% in 2013; P(late | previous leg late) = 0.79 against 0.11; the literature's most common error is a random split.

**Decision.** Rolling-origin evaluation 2006-2013 is the headline result; the fixed split 2002-2010 / 2011 / 2012-2013 is illustrative. Two horizons reported separately: day-ahead (D-1) and at-gate (H-1, with the previous leg's actual delay). Metrics: AUC, PR-AUC, Brier, calibration. Leakage tests are part of the test suite: nothing from the flight after departure, no same-period aggregate containing the flight, historical windows closed before the flight date.

## ADR-0010 — Governance of divergences (2026-09-05)

**Decision.** When two implementation options diverge and the evidence does not settle it, a panel of independent reviewers evaluates both and the recommended option is adopted; the divergence and the panel's reasoning are recorded here.

## ADR-0011 — Airline classes include `other` (2026-09-05)

**Decision.** `groups.csv` carries four classes: FSC, LCC, regional and other. `other` covers carriers with scheduled domestic legs that are neither incumbents, low-cost nor regional by business model (foreign carriers' domestic legs, charter or cargo operators appearing under N/R/E, small operators without a public profile). Unlabelled codes are treated as `other`. Amends ADR-0003, whose "the rest" now reads `other`. Class follows the absorbing group after a merger.

## ADR-0012 — Missing actual times in the legacy files (2026-09-05)

**Basis.** In the 2000-2009 raw files a realised flight with no reported occurrence has empty actual departure and arrival times. The authors' convention, on which the article's tables rest, read an empty actual time as on time (actual = scheduled, delay 0); the sign of a measured delay follows ADR-0008.

**Decision.** The staged data keeps nulls and signed delays. Delay computations take a named parameter `empty_actual_means_on_time`: `True` for the reconstruction panel, so that it follows the article's convention, declared in the registry; `False` everywhere else, including the prediction dataset. The share of realised flights with missing actual times is reported per year. Amended on the prediction side by ADR-0017.

## ADR-0013 — `fsc_*` columns use the article's carrier set (2026-09-05)

**Basis.** The article's FSC set is the TAM group, the Varig group until 2007-03, Transbrasil and Vasp; the class-based set of ADR-0003 also includes Avianca Brasil. The two sets measure different things and both are worth publishing.

**Decision.** The reconstruction panel's `fsc_*` family uses the article's carrier set; the class-based family is published as the variant `fscc_*`. Supersedes the naming in the phase-3 brief.

## ADR-0014 — Analysis tables are tracked in git (2026-09-05)

**Decision.** `data/analysis/*.parquet` and `*.csv.gz` below 50 MB are tracked (the two panels and the fact table are 2-12 MB), so a reader can run the estimation and the tables without rebuilding. The reconstruction tables are regenerated by `just fact && just panel` (about 11 s and 8 s on the full series) and checked for staleness against the registry by `just check-analysis`. Flight-level data remain outside git (ADR-0004).

## ADR-0015 — Symmetric outlier rule and suspect actual times (2026-09-05)

**Evidence.** The raw files carry month typos in actual times: for VSP 4374 in December 2003 the actual arrival is dated November, giving -43,170 minutes, and 133 route-months of the reconstruction panel had |FSC minutes| above 200 (minimum -4,772). The one-sided cut inherited from the authors' scripts (`delay < 313.25`) lets every negative typo through.

**Decision.** Amends ADR-0008: the outlier threshold applies to the absolute value of the delay for every sum, mean and share of minutes (|delay| < 313.25 by default). Staging adds a boolean `actual_time_suspect` when |departure or arrival delay| >= 1,440 minutes, and the prediction dataset excludes those flights from targets, counted per year. Counts of delayed flights (thresholds 0, 15, 30, 60) are unaffected except for the suspect flag.

## ADR-0016 — Key uniqueness is a tested invariant (2026-09-05)

**Evidence.** The first reconstruction panel carried 866 duplicated route-month rows and the fact table 422 duplicated group-route-month keys, with off-universe counts differing between copies.

**Decision.** `(group, route, ym)` in the fact table, `(route, ym)` in the panel and `(city, ym)` in the city tables are unique by construction and by test; the aggregation functions assert it and CI runs the check on the committed tables.

## ADR-0017 — Empty actual times mean "no alteration reported" (2026-09-05, panel of three reviewers)

**Divergence.** ADR-0012 applied "empty actual time = on time" to the reconstruction panel only and "empty = unknown" to the prediction dataset. Under the second reading only 4,965,966 of 10,200,578 scheduled flights had an arrival target and the surviving pre-2010 flights were 75-94% late.

**Panel (ADR-0010).** Three independent reviewers, one instructed to be sceptical, read IAC 1504, the prediction outputs and the evidence then available on the 2000-2009 files. Verdicts recorded in `docs/notes/colegiado-adr0012.md`. Majority: reading B.

**Basis.** IAC 1504 defines an exception system: the "Boletim de Alteração de Vôo" is issued only "sempre que houver alguma alteração" (introduction, §3.1); actual times and the cause code are fields of the BAV (§4.2 n, o, p); the SITAR is filled "para todas as alterações verificadas" (§5.1); annex 2 has no code for "operated on schedule". Under reading B the delay rate is 20.3% in 2002-2005 and 26.8% in 2006-2010 against 21% and 30% in the published Table 1 (article, Table 1 -- outside document); under reading A, 75-94%.

**Decision.** Supersedes the prediction half of ADR-0012. For files of the 2000-2009 layout, a realised flight with empty actual times operated with no reported alteration: delays are 0 and a boolean `on_time_no_bav` marks the row (scope: `is_realized` and layout year < 2010; the residual nulls of 2010+ stay null). The prediction dataset, its targets and its lagged aggregates are rebuilt under this reading; a sensitivity line reports the headline metrics under reading A. Documented in `docs/notes/prediction.md`: reading B is a floor on punctuality (an unreported delay counts as on time), the cause code is empty for the same reason, and the 2010 layout change remains a comparability break. The reconstruction panel is unchanged.

**Scope amendment after the dissenting review (same date).** The sceptical reviewer measured, for 2005, null actual-arrival rates by carrier from 0% (small regionals) to 90-100% for foreign carriers and code-share legs (TPU 100%, AVA 99.5%, ACA 98.8%, DAL 94.2%, AAL 94.9%, UAL 90.6%), with the domestic majors in between (TAM 71.8%, GLO 64.4%, VRG 57.0%), and cited IAC 1504 §6.6: in code-share only the operating carrier reports and the non-operator's leg has no effect on the indices. Reading B is therefore applied only to realised flights of carriers whose class in `groups.csv` is FSC, LCC or regional; for `other` and unlabelled carriers empty actual times stay unknown and those flights stay out of delay targets and shares. The null rate by carrier and year is published in `reports/prediction/null_actual_by_carrier.csv` (`scripts/null_actual_by_carrier.py`). A separate audit will decide whether non-operating code-share legs should leave the universes altogether (candidate ADR-0018).

ADR-0018 is reserved for that code-share audit and has not been written.

## ADR-0019 — Theory layer: the 2013 monograph's congestion economics and Stackelberg model, derived and documented (2026-09-05)

**Context.** The article this repository replicates rests on a theory it does not derive: that airlines with market power internalise part of the congestion they cause, and that a Stackelberg leader facing a follower internalises less of it than a Cournot duopolist. The author worked that theory out in the 2013 undergraduate monograph *Efeitos da entrada de uma empresa aérea de baixo custo na internalização das externalidades do congestionamento* (USP). Its section 2 reviews the economics of airport congestion after Cohen and Coughlin (2003) and Cohen, Coughlin and Ott (2009), its section 3 the internalisation literature, its section 4 follows Brueckner and Van Dender (2008) for a leader with one follower, and its sections 5-7 estimate a weighted fixed-effects panel on a regression base of its own. The equations were embedded objects and the five figures are the two Federal Reserve papers' figures. Guo, Jiang and Wan (2018, *Transportation Research Part A* 118, 648-661) later took the internalisation term of the same game to airfares, building explicitly on the 2016 article.

**Evidence.** Re-deriving equations (1)-(12) symbolically (`src/airline_delays/theory/model.py`, printed to `reports/theory/model.json`, 29 identities pinned by `tests/test_theory.py`) reproduces the monograph's statements and sharpens three of them: the leader operates f1 = f2/(1-λ) ≥ 2 f2 flights, not merely f1 > f2 (from equations (7) and (9) at an interior equilibrium with the same fare, tax and seats for both carriers and perfectly elastic demand; equality under linear cost); with linear congestion cost the leader's toll at the symmetric optimum is exactly three quarters of the marginal congestion damage, halfway between the Cournot and the atomistic toll, which the monograph's "entre a metade da tarifa de Cournot e a tarifa atomística" garbles in translation; and under inelastic demand the follower's reaction slope stays within (-1, -1/2] only if c''/s ≥ s²d'', a condition neither the monograph nor its source states. The monograph's unit of observation (airline x route x month, delays over 30 minutes) is this repository's fact-table grain (ADR-0004), its frequency-based HHI is `rthhi_flights`, its six airline groups carry the merger dates ADR-0003 adopted, and its 38 listed airports cover the 27 nodes of ADR-0001 (`data/external/monograph_airports.csv`); its weather, seat and connecting-passenger variables are sources 3, 9 and 13 of `docs/data-availability.md`, not collected.

**Decision.** (1) A theory layer exists: `src/airline_delays/theory/` (MIT, sympy; figures drawn with matplotlib in the SAPIANS scientific style, vendored with attribution in `src/airline_delays/theory/sapians_style.py` — an active insight title, a subtitle with the parameters, direct labels, the SAPIANS palette); `airline-delays theory` (`just theory`) writes `reports/theory/model.json`, `figures.json`, eleven SVG figures (the five diagrams of the monograph's section 2 and six on the game and the bridge) and `results.md` without a timestamp, and `tests/test_theory.py` pins every identity and fails when the committed report is stale. (2) Its documentation is Portuguese (ADR-0006) and thematic, not chronological: the theory chapters of the study under `docs/study/` (layout amended by ADR-0022), `docs/notes/monografia-2013.md` as the evidence note, and the study report `reports/study.typ` — a SAPIANS report built on the design package vendored under `reports/sapians/` (MIT copy of `sapians-latex` v0.1.0) — the game formulated from players, strategies and timing and derived step by step. (3) Derived and restated are labelled apart: equations (1)-(12) are derived here; Proposition 1 and "Pressuposições" 2 and 3 are restated from Brueckner and Van Dender (2008) and the monograph, quoted literally and attributed; what the derivation adds, including the low-cost entrant extension and the "three margins of internalisation" reading, is marked as this repository's. (4) The figures are redrawn from parametric curves whose parameters are in `figures.json`; nothing is copied from the source papers or the monograph. (5) The monograph is quoted in short literal excerpts and cited as an outside document until the author's Zenodo deposit mints a DOI, which then enters `CITATION.cff` under `references`; the regression base behind its Tables 5 and 6 is not redistributed and is read by nothing here. (6) The econometrics of reference remains the 2016 article's; the monograph's equation (22) is not re-estimated (`docs/notes/monografia-2013.md`), and its own estimates are quoted as external numbers. (7) Every number and inequality in the new prose is printed by `src/airline_delays/theory/run.py` or by `scripts/monograph_airports.py`, or is marked as coming from an outside document; the rule of `CLAUDE.md` applies to algebra as it does to counts. (8) Literature is cited inline author-year, with the single full list in `docs/study/bibliografia.md` and a DOI only where it has been resolved. (9) ADR-0018 remains reserved for the code-share audit.

## ADR-0020 — The article's estimation panel is published (2026-09-05)

**Context.** Tables 2-7 of the article were estimated on a route x month panel the authors closed in December 2015: a Stata file of 24,589 route-months and 1,829 variables, header timestamp 3 Dec 2015. The author of this repository is the article's first author and holds that panel. Until now it stayed outside the repository, and the reconstruction was compared with it indirectly through agreement rates.

**Decision.** (1) The panel is curated once, by `airline-delays article-panel`, and published as `data/analysis/article_panel_route_month.parquet` and `.csv.gz` under CC BY 4.0: keys and geography, the flight counts every share is built on, the six regressands, the nine exogenous regressors, the two concentration terms with the alternative city term, the seven instruments and the components of the two low-cost dummies -- 52 columns, every one declared in the registry (layer `article_panel`, `src/airline_delays/schema/columns.py`) and in `datapackage.json`. The generated route, time and seasonality dummies are rebuilt by code (`src/airline_delays/estimation/loader.py`) and not shipped; no column from a non-open source ships; `data/analysis/article_panel_manifest.json` records the source's sha256 and header timestamp, the per-column null counts and the hashes of both files, never a path. (2) It is the single estimation input of `airline-delays estimate`, which writes `reports/replication/`. The scorecard of the re-estimated tables against the published ones is the replication result; the published and the re-estimated sample sizes are both reported, and no filter is reconstructed to close the gap between them. (3) The reconstruction panel is a separate product -- the open extended dataset and the prediction input -- related to the article's panel by the shared universe, node map and carrier sets (ADR-0001, ADR-0002, ADR-0003 with ADR-0013, ADR-0005) and by the article's empty-actual-time convention (ADR-0012), not by a verification; where the article states no rule the reconstruction declares its own (ADR-0008, ADR-0015) and publishes its congestion, concentration and low-cost measures under their own names (ADR-0007). (4) Supersedes the private-data policy and every agreement-rate artefact and sentence. The evidence sentences of ADR-0001, ADR-0002, ADR-0005, ADR-0012, ADR-0013 and ADR-0017 are struck, and the decisions stand as the article's definitions, restated by its first author. (5) Rights: the first author holds the final base, curated it and releases the panel on their own responsibility; the article's three authors are credited in every citation of the panel as the authors of the underlying research; upstream ANAC data (VRA, tariff microdata, statistical data) are attributed, not relicensed.

## ADR-0021 — One stage-ordered package, one narrative, two languages (2026-09-05)

**Decision.** (1) Code lives in `src/airline_delays/`, with the subpackages `ingest`, `staging`, `reference`, `fact`, `panel`, `estimation`, `prediction`, `theory` and `reporting` in pipeline order, plus the cross-cutting `definitions/` and `schema/`. The CLI is `airline-delays <stage>`; the `justfile` recipes follow the same order. The name `vra` and the two names of the retired verification layer disappear from the repository. (2) `reports/summary.json`, written by `airline-delays summary`, is the only source of numbers quoted in the entry pages; `scripts/check_prose_numbers.py` enforces it. (3) The entry layer is bilingual: `README.md` (English, the main page) with `README.pt-BR.md`; `docs/README.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md` and `data/README.md` with their `.pt-BR` counterparts -- kept in parity by `scripts/check_readme_parity.py`. The tutorial, the theory chapters, the notes and the Typst reports stay in Portuguese (ADR-0006), with an English summary paragraph on each index page. (4) The vocabulary of the retired layer does not appear (`tests/test_prose_vocabulary.py`). (5) No universe, threshold, taxonomy, carrier set or reported number changes: the committed reconstruction tables were regenerated byte-identically, and every re-estimated coefficient equals the previous run. (6) Publication metadata has one source, `src/airline_delays/schema/metadata.py`, from which `datapackage.json` and `.zenodo.json` are generated and against which `CITATION.cff` and `pyproject.toml` are tested; the first publication is tagged v1.0.0, and Zenodo archives the release and mints the DOI once the integration is active. (7) `CHANGELOG.md` is consolidated.

## ADR-0022 — The study replaces the tutorial and the theory chapters; the PDFs are tracked (2026-09-05)

**Context.** `docs/tutorial/` told the research arc of the article as its first author lived it — proposal, research designs, the road to the article, peer review, reception — in fifteen modules, and `docs/theory/` held the theory apart from the article. The owner asked for one final work instead: the microeconomics of congestion, the game-theory model and the article — its data, specification, results, replication and reception — as a single, organised study with nothing biographical in it; for the equations to render on GitHub, whose Markdown parser consumes asterisks, thin spaces and paired subscripts inside bare `$...$` before MathJax runs; and for the compiled report, until now git-ignored, to stay in the repository.

**Decision.** (1) `docs/study/` is the study: an opening, eight chapters in three parts (I the economics of airport congestion; II game theory, from the fundamentals to the Stackelberg congestion model derived step by step; III the article — from the model to the hypotheses, the data, specification and identification, results and replication, reception), four appendices (the delay predictor, how to reproduce, rights and licences, extensions) and the bibliography, indexed by `docs/study/README.md`. `docs/tutorial/` and `docs/theory/` no longer exist; the content that survives moved with its history and was rewritten. (2) Biography is out of scope. The 2013 monograph is cited as an outside document — "the author's 2013 undergraduate monograph (USP)" — and nothing about its supervision, the author's later graduate studies or the article's peer review appears anywhere in the repository; `tests/test_prose_vocabulary.py` enforces the words and the retired paths. (3) Mathematics in Markdown follows GitHub's renderer: inline math as $`...`$ code-span math, display math inside ```math fences, one equation per fence, `\tag{n}` for numbering. `scripts/check_markdown_math.py` rejects bare `$...$` and `$$`, a table not preceded by a blank line and an unclosed fence; it runs in `just check` and in the `docs-paths` CI job. (4) The three Typst reports compile into the tracked `reports/pdf/` — `study.pdf` from `reports/study.typ` (formerly the theory report, extended with the article's part), `replication.pdf` and `prediction.pdf` — with the release date of `CITATION.cff` as creation timestamp and printed date, so a rerun on an unchanged tree rewrites identical bytes and the PDFs change only with the prose or a release. (5) Every number in the study is a value of `reports/summary.json`, `reports/theory/model.json`, `reports/replication/` or `src/airline_delays/estimation/published.json`, or is marked as an outside document; the discrete game of the primer is written into `reports/theory/model.json` by `airline-delays theory` and pinned by `tests/test_theory.py`, so the rule of `CLAUDE.md` holds for the teaching example too. (6) ADR-0019 (2) is amended to the new layout; no definition in the code and no reported number changes.
